# Job search how to

How do you know if your target company is open to hiring (tech) talent from abroad?

Here are some tips from our team:

1️⃣ The first (and simplest) thing you can do is to scan its Careers page and/or vacancies for keywords such as “visa” and “relocation.”

2️⃣ Look through the list of its employees on LinkedIn (https://derrick-app.com/en/find-company-employees-linkedin/). If you see foreign hires among them, it’s a good sign. Also, foreigner-friendly companies often emphasize on their website/social media profiles/vacancy pages that they have an international team, employees from n+ countries, etc.

3️⃣ Pay attention to the language. If a job opening is in the local language and/or requires knowledge of the local language, it is most likely that the company behind it is not (actively) hiring from abroad. Jobs open to international applicants are usually in English, and knowledge of the local language is a plus, not a requirement.

4️⃣ Search for relevant public lists. For instance, the official website of the Dutch Immigration and Naturalization Service (IND) offers a list of the Netherlands-based companies-recognized sponsors of the Highly Skilled Migrant permit (https://ind.nl/en/public-register-recognised-sponsors). On GOV.UK, you can find a list of UK employers licensed to sponsor a Skilled Worker visa (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-workers). Don’t be lazy, just Google.

5️⃣ Check out our International Job Search Handbook (https://github.com/AndrewStetsenko/tech-jobs-with-relocation) on GitHub. We keep there a list of companies hiring internationally. By the way, if you haven’t already, give the repository a ⭐️ star.

And finally, we’d like to remind you that we have a blog post (https://relocate.me/blog/job-search/how-to-find-unadvertised-tech-jobs/) covering finding unadvertised yet very alive international job opps by using good old Google 🔍 and Boolean search operators.
